BPC-157 Benefits: A Research Overview

Research reference · Updated September 2026 · ~6 min read

BPC-157, sometimes called Body Protection Compound 157, is a synthetic peptide based on a sequence found in human gastric juice. It is among the most researched peptides for tissue repair. This overview summarizes the areas of interest reported in the literature, framed strictly as research.

Tendon and ligament research

A large share of BPC-157 studies focus on connective tissue. Animal research has examined its effect on tendon-to-bone healing and ligament repair, with attention to how it may influence the migration of tendon fibroblasts and the formation of new blood vessels. This vascular angle, called angiogenesis, is a recurring theme in the research.

Gut and digestive research

Given its origin in gastric juice, BPC-157 has been studied extensively in the digestive tract. Research has looked at intestinal integrity and the stomach lining. This is one of the oldest lines of BPC-157 investigation and part of why it carries the protective compound name.

Other reported areas

  • Muscle tissue and recovery after injury in animal models
  • Interaction with the nitric oxide pathway
  • Possible protective effects on the gut-brain axis in preclinical work
Important context: the strongest BPC-157 evidence is preclinical, meaning animal and laboratory studies. Human clinical trials are limited, so these are research observations, not proven human outcomes.

Why researchers pair it carefully with dosing

Reported outcomes in protocols depend heavily on consistent dosing. The dosage chart collects the common ranges, and the protocol page covers method. Anyone reviewing benefits should also read the side effect overview for balance.

The bottom line

BPC-157 is a compelling research target for connective tissue and gut studies, with a broad preclinical base and limited human data. Treat any benefit claim as a research hypothesis rather than a settled fact.
