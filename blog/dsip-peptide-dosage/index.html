DSIP Peptide Dosage: Delta Sleep-Inducing Peptide Guide

Research reference · Updated September 2026 · ~6 min read

DSIP, or delta sleep-inducing peptide, is a naturally occurring peptide studied for its links to sleep regulation and stress. This guide collects the reported dosing ranges into a reference chart. Figures are research-reported and not dosing advice.

What DSIP is studied for

DSIP was first identified for its association with delta wave sleep, the deep stage of the sleep cycle. Research has looked at its possible role in sleep patterns, stress response, and hormonal regulation. Human data is limited, so it remains a research compound.

DSIP is dosed in micrograms and is usually placed in the evening to align with its sleep research context. Convert micrograms to syringe units with the dosage calculator.

Timing is the theme: because DSIP is studied in the context of sleep, protocols almost always schedule the dose shortly before the intended sleep window.

Handling notes

DSIP is reconstituted with bacteriostatic water and stored refrigerated like other peptides. See the reconstitution guide for the method. As a peptide with limited human data, quality of material matters, so use tested sources from the supplier list.

What is not established

DSIP research is early and mixed. Its exact mechanism is still debated, and long term human safety is unknown. Treat any sleep-related observation as a research hypothesis rather than a proven effect.
