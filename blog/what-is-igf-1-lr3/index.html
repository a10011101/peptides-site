What Is IGF-1 LR3?

Research reference · Updated November 2026 · ~8 min read

IGF-1 LR3 is a laboratory analog of insulin-like growth factor 1. The short name describes the edits, not a brand. Native IGF-1 is 70 amino acids. LR3 adds a 13-residue extension at the N-terminus ("Long") and replaces glutamic acid with arginine at position 3 ("R3"). The result is an 83-amino-acid peptide with a molecular weight of about 9117 daltons. Researchers use it when they want an IGF-1 receptor signal that is not soaked up by IGF binding proteins. This page is a research reference only. It is not a dosing guide and not medical advice.

Why the sequence was changed

Most IGF-1 in circulation is not free. It sits on IGF binding proteins, especially IGFBP-3, often inside a larger complex with the acid-labile subunit. That binding keeps a reserve of hormone in blood for many hours. The free fraction clears in roughly 10 to 20 minutes, which is the window the IGF-1 LR3 protocol rounds to about 15 minutes for native IGF-1.

LR3 was built to bind those proteins poorly. Both the arginine substitution and the N-terminal extension cut IGFBP affinity, while binding to the IGF-1 receptor stays in a similar range to the native peptide. Less of the analog is parked on carrier proteins, so a larger share stays available to the receptor. Characterization studies put the circulating half-life near 20 to 30 hours, the figure listed on that protocol page. The longer free window is the point of the analog. It is also why LR3 should not be described as a slow-release copy of a pharmacy IGF-1 product.

Mecasermin, sold as Increlex, is recombinant human IGF-1 with the native sequence. Regulators approved it for severe primary IGF-1 deficiency, a rare condition in children. IGF-1 LR3 is a different molecule. It is not that drug, and it has no matching approval for any human use.

What the signal does in research models

The IGF-1 receptor is a receptor tyrosine kinase, closely related to the insulin receptor. Once LR3 binds it, PI3K-Akt-mTOR supports protein synthesis and cell survival, and the Ras-MAPK path supports proliferation. In skeletal-muscle models, investigators follow satellite-cell activity and, in some animal studies, both larger fibers and a rise in fiber number.

At higher concentrations the analog can also engage the insulin receptor. Native IGF-1 does this when binding proteins are not buffering it. Because LR3 stays free, research notes treat glucose drops as more likely. Reported observations, including glucose changes, fluid shifts, and local irritation, sit on the IGF-1 LR3 side effects page. Long occupancy also downregulates the receptor, so research logs usually describe a limited window of about four weeks rather than continuous exposure. The before-and-after notes separate measured animal and imaging results from anecdote.

How it differs from a growth hormone secretagogue

CJC-1295 and ipamorelin sit upstream of IGF-1. They are studied as signals for the pituitary to release growth hormone, after which the liver can make native IGF-1. LR3 skips that path and occupies the IGF-1 receptor itself. A secretagogue still depends on the pituitary and liver. LR3 does not, and it does not preserve the pulse those peptides are meant to study. A fair comparison starts with what CJC-1295 does and the protocol index. For the growth hormone pulse itself, the ipamorelin protocol is the closer read.

Where the dosage question actually lives

Research logs cited on this site cluster reported amounts in the tens of micrograms, often for about four weeks, then time off. Those figures are research-reported, not dosing advice. Ranges and cautions are on the IGF-1 LR3 dosage chart. Read them as a record, not as a plan for personal use.

What a careful reading should not skip

IGF-1 signaling is mitogenic. It tells susceptible cells to grow and divide, which is why the analog is used in proliferation assays and why study designs keep existing malignancy, unexplained tissue growth, and pregnancy models out of scope. Human trials of the kind required for a medical claim have not been done for LR3. Animal work has reported a stronger muscle signal than native IGF-1, attributed to longer receptor occupancy and lower IGFBP binding. That does not close the human-evidence gap.

Purity is part of identity. Material without HPLC and mass spectrometry is not confirmed IGF-1 LR3, and contaminant reactions get blamed on the peptide. Third-party tested lots are the standard behind the supplier list. The same bar applies to compounds such as BPC-157. A name on a label is not an identity test.

Short answer

IGF-1 LR3 is an 83-amino-acid research analog of IGF-1 with weak IGFBP binding and a research-reported half-life near 20 to 30 hours. It is a receptor ligand for cell and animal work. It is not approved for human consumption, not a substitute for mecasermin, and not a growth hormone secretagogue.
