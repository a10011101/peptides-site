CJC-1295 for Muscle Growth: Research Overview

Research reference · Updated September 2026 · ~6 min read

CJC-1295 is frequently discussed in the context of lean mass because it raises growth hormone, which in turn raises IGF-1. This overview explains how the mechanism is studied and why it is usually paired with another peptide. Research information only.

The mechanism

CJC-1295 is a GHRH analog. GHRH is the natural signal that tells the pituitary to release growth hormone. By mimicking that signal with a longer half-life, CJC-1295 is studied for its ability to increase the amount and duration of growth hormone pulses. Growth hormone then drives IGF-1, the hormone most associated with tissue growth.

Why it is paired with ipamorelin

CJC-1295 and ipamorelin act on two different receptors. CJC-1295 amplifies the GHRH pathway while ipamorelin, a ghrelin mimetic, triggers release through a separate channel and suppresses the effect less. Used together they are studied for a larger, cleaner growth hormone pulse than either alone. The dosing structure is in the stack dosage guide.

Realistic framing: research interest in lean mass does not equal proven muscle gain in humans. Growth hormone secretagogues produce more modest and slower changes than exogenous growth hormone, and human data is limited.

What the research examines

  • Growth hormone pulse amplitude and frequency
  • Downstream IGF-1 levels
  • Recovery and body composition markers in preclinical models

Dosing and timing

Because growth hormone release follows a daily rhythm, protocols often place doses at night or after fasting windows. The specifics are on the CJC-1295 protocol page. Review the side effect overview before starting any research block.
